This is a very good opportunity because you are not just asking “what should I do next?” You are implicitly asking: **how can the organisation get maximum strategic value from a rare, well-funded, independently led research platform?**

I would go in with 3 aims:

1. **Understand how ExCo thinks about strategic research leadership.**
2. **Position your fellowship as an organisational asset, not just your personal grant.**
3. **Explore how translation beyond fusion could fit the organisation’s mission and risk appetite.**

Here are some strong questions you could choose from.

## Opening framing

You might start with something like:

> “I’m 18 months into the fellowship and it is going very well. I’m starting to think seriously about what comes after the first four years: not just in terms of my own career, but how the organisation can use this platform to build strategic capability, generate wider scientific and economic benefit, and translate technology beyond our core fusion mission. I’d really value your perspective on where you think the biggest opportunities and gaps are.”

That frames the meeting at the right level.

## Questions about organisational strategy

These are good early questions because they invite the director to talk from their ExCo vantage point.

**1. “From your perspective on ExCo, what are the most important strategic capabilities our division needs to build over the next 5–10 years?”**

This gets you out of project-level thinking and into capability-level thinking.

**2. “Where do you see the biggest gap between our scientific strengths and our ability to translate them into economic or industrial impact?”**

This is especially relevant given the mission statement. It also opens the door to discussing structures, incentives, partnerships, IP, commercialisation, and external engagement.

**3. “Are there areas where you think we are scientifically strong but under-recognised externally?”**

This could help you identify where your fellowship might help raise profile, build networks, or create a new strategic theme.

**4. “What does ExCo currently see as the organisation’s role in technologies adjacent to, or outside, fusion?”**

This is a very useful question if you are thinking about non-core industrial impact. It probes whether translation beyond fusion is genuinely wanted, merely tolerated, or strategically important.

## Questions about your fellowship as a strategic platform

These are more directly about you, but framed through organisational value.

**5. “How should I be thinking about the fellowship as an organisational asset rather than just an individual research programme?”**

This is probably one of the best questions you can ask. It signals maturity and ambition without sounding self-serving.

**6. “What would make the fellowship a clear strategic success from the organisation’s point of view by the end of year four?”**

This helps you learn what senior leadership actually values: papers, capability, people, partnerships, IP, spinouts, policy influence, international reputation, industrial income, recruitment, or something else.

**7. “Are there organisational priorities where you think my fellowship could have a disproportionate impact if I aligned it correctly now?”**

This invites them to connect your work to higher-level objectives.

**8. “What should I be careful not to miss while I still have this period of independent funding and flexibility?”**

This is a subtle but powerful question. It may reveal opportunities that are only available because of your current position.

## Questions about strategic research leadership

Since you are thinking about what comes next, I would ask explicitly about leadership, but not in a “how do I get promoted?” way.

**9. “What does strategic research leadership look like in our organisation at the level above leading successful projects?”**

This is excellent because many research organisations are bad at defining the transition from excellent PI to strategic leader.

**10. “What capabilities do you think I should deliberately develop over the next 2–3 years if I want to lead a broader research agenda?”**

This gives them permission to advise you on gaps: stakeholder management, influencing, commercial awareness, programme leadership, policy awareness, team building, financial responsibility, or external representation.

**11. “Who in the organisation is currently very good at turning technical depth into strategic influence, and what could I learn from them?”**

This may lead to introductions, mentors, or models of leadership that are more useful than abstract advice.

**12. “Where do early-career or mid-career research leaders typically struggle when they try to step into more strategic roles here?”**

This is a nice way of asking, “What traps should I avoid?”

## Questions about translation and industrial impact

Given your interest in applications outside fusion, I would definitely include a few of these.

**13. “How much appetite does the organisation have for translating technologies into sectors beyond our core industry, where there is a credible route to economic impact?”**

This directly tests strategic permission.

**14. “What would a good translation pathway look like here: licensing, spinouts, industrial partnerships, collaborative R&D, standards, consultancy, or something else?”**

This question is practical and lets them reveal which routes the organisation actually supports.

**15. “What are the main barriers inside the organisation that stop promising research from becoming industrial impact?”**

This might uncover things like IP processes, risk appetite, lack of business development capacity, incentives, procurement constraints, partner access, or unclear ownership.

**16. “If I wanted to explore industrial translation seriously, who should I be working with internally from the beginning?”**

This is a good action-oriented question. You may come away with names.

**17. “How do we decide when an opportunity outside fusion is strategically aligned, rather than a distraction?”**

This is a very insightful question for an arms-length body. It acknowledges mission discipline while still exploring broader impact.

## Questions about influence and governance

Because you are talking to someone on ExCo, use that perspective.

**18. “What kinds of evidence or arguments are most persuasive at ExCo when making the case for a new strategic research direction?”**

This is extremely useful if you may later pitch a programme, centre, platform, or translation initiative.

**19. “At what point should an emerging research area be surfaced to divisional or executive leadership?”**

This helps you understand timing. Too early and it looks speculative; too late and you miss strategic alignment.

**20. “How can someone in my position engage with strategy without bypassing normal management structures?”**

This is politically intelligent. It shows you understand governance and do not want to create friction.

## Questions about people, teams, and legacy

These matter because strategic leadership is usually about building capability through others.

**21. “What kind of team or capability would you hope exists at the end of my fellowship that does not exist today?”**

This converts your fellowship from a grant into a legacy.

**22. “How should I balance delivering the fellowship objectives with building a broader platform that can outlast the fellowship?”**

This is likely very relevant to your situation.

**23. “Are there particular skills or roles we are missing that would unlock more value from this research area?”**

This can lead to discussion about recruitment, technical staff, software engineering, commercialisation support, project management, or partnerships.

## Questions about external positioning

Useful if your work is going well and could raise the organisation’s profile.

**24. “Where should we be more visible externally if we want to be seen as a leader in this area?”**

**25. “Are there strategic partners — industry, academia, government, or international labs — that you think we should be building stronger relationships with?”**

**26. “How does the organisation want to be perceived by funders like UKRI over the next few years?”**

That last one is particularly relevant because you already hold a major UKRI fellowship.

## A strong final question

I would end with something like:

**“If you were in my position, 18 months into a well-funded fellowship with strong momentum, what would you do over the next year to maximise both scientific impact and strategic value to the organisation?”**

That is personal, strategic, and likely to produce useful advice.

## My suggested top 8 for the actual meeting

For a half-hour conversation, I would not try to ask too many. I would prioritise these:

1. **From your ExCo perspective, what strategic capabilities does our division most need to build over the next 5–10 years?**
2. **How should I think about the fellowship as an organisational asset, not just an individual research programme?**
3. **What would make the fellowship a strategic success from the organisation’s point of view by the end of year four?**
4. **What does strategic research leadership look like here beyond leading successful projects?**
5. **How much appetite is there for translating technologies into sectors beyond fusion where there is credible economic impact?**
6. **What are the main internal barriers that stop promising research becoming industrial impact?**
7. **Who should I be building relationships with now if I want to explore translation seriously and responsibly?**
8. **If you were in my position, what would you focus on over the next 12 months?**

## One thing I would avoid

I would avoid making the meeting too explicitly about your next job title, promotion route, or future role. Those things matter, but the more powerful framing is:

> “How can I use this unusual platform to build something strategically valuable for the organisation?”

That will probably make the career conversation happen naturally, but at a higher and more useful level.
