Title:
pyvale: Image-Based Experimental Design in the Virtual Engineering Laboratory

Presenter:
Dr Lloyd Fletcher
Principal Research Engineer & UKRI Future Leaders Fellow
UK Atomic Energy Authority

Abstract:
Fusion power components operate in one of the harshest engineering environments imaginable, facing extreme thermal loads, large mechanical forces, and intense electromagnetic fields. Validating these components experimentally is therefore costly, slow, and data-hungry. The real question is not simply how to run these experiments, but how to design them intelligently so that every test delivers maximum risk reduction for minimum cost. This seminar introduces pyvale (the python validation engine), an open-source virtual engineering laboratory for sensor simulation, uncertainty quantification, experimental design, sensor placement optimisation, and simulation calibration/validation. Focusing on the imaging side of the toolbox, I will show how virtual cameras, digital image correlation, and fast synthetic measurement simulation can be used to design improved experiments before they are deployed in the laboratory. The aim of pyvale is to reduce the cost of validation by turning expensive physical campaigns into simulation-guided, uncertainty-aware, data-rich experiments. I will draw examples from high heat flux testing of fusion divertor component mock-ups instrumented with thermal and mechanical diagnostics.

Bio:
Lloyd Fletcher is a Principal Research Engineer and UKRI Future Leaders Fellow at the UK Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA). His research focuses on simulation-driven experimental design, image-based mechanics, uncertainty quantification, and digital validation methods for high-value fusion engineering systems. He completed his doctorate at the University of Adelaide, Australia, before moving to the University of Southampton, where he worked in high strain-rate materials testing, held a Leverhulme Early Career Fellowship, and later became a Lecturer in Aerostructures. He joined UKAEA in 2021, where he has built a research team developing data-rich approaches for validating fusion components under coupled thermal, mechanical, and electromagnetic loading. His current UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship focuses on creating a computer-aided validation framework for optimising the design of large-scale experiments, with particular emphasis on virtual sensing, imaging diagnostics, and uncertainty-aware validation for fusion engineering.
