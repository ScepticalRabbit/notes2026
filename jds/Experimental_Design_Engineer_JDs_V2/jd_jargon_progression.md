# JD Jargon Progression: Experimental Design Engineer Grades E–K

This controlled vocabulary is intended to make progression between grades explicit and
consistent across the Experimental Design Engineer job family. It should be used in
accountabilities, qualifications and experience requirements without stacking several
near-synonyms in the same sentence.

## 1. Knowledge and Experience Progression

| Grade | Knowledge depth | Knowledge breadth | Experience depth | Experience breadth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | Demonstrable | Developing | Practical | Relevant |
| F | Strong | Growing | Established | Broader |
| G | Advanced | Broad | Substantial | Wide |
| H | Deep | Extensive | Extensive | Multidisciplinary |
| I | Authoritative | Comprehensive | Sustained and significant | Integrated and strategic |
| J | Strategic and authoritative | Organisation-wide | Extensive and sustained | Cross-programme and organisational |
| K | Internationally recognised authority | Field-shaping | Distinguished and sustained | National and international |

### Preferred grade-level phrases

- **E:** Demonstrable knowledge and practical experience, with developing breadth.
- **F:** Strong knowledge and established experience, with growing breadth.
- **G:** Advanced knowledge and substantial experience, with broad capability.
- **H:** Deep knowledge and extensive experience, with multidisciplinary breadth.
- **I:** Authoritative knowledge and sustained, significant experience, with
  comprehensive and integrated breadth.
- **J:** Strategic and authoritative knowledge with extensive, sustained leadership
  experience across programmes and organisational capabilities.
- **K:** Internationally recognised authority with distinguished, sustained experience
  shaping the field nationally and internationally.

## 2. Task Complexity Progression

| Grade | Task complexity |
|---|---|
| E | Moderately complex |
| F | Moderately complex and variable |
| G | Complex and multifaceted |
| H | Highly complex and multidisciplinary |
| I | Significant, complex and strategically important |
| J | Major, highly ambiguous and organisation-shaping |
| K | Unprecedented, field-defining and strategically critical |

## 3. Precedent and Novelty Progression

| Grade | Degree of precedent | Typical novelty |
|---|---|---|
| E | Clear precedent and established methods | Applies and implements |
| F | Established precedent requiring interpretation or adaptation | Selects and adapts |
| G | Partial precedent; established methods may not fully fit | Defines and develops |
| H | Limited precedent; significant method development required | Integrates and innovates |
| I | Limited or emerging precedent; novel integration often required | Establishes and shapes |
| J | Little precedent across programmes or organisational capability | Creates and directs |
| K | Little or no precedent nationally or internationally | Originates and defines the field |

## 4. Freedom to Act and Judgement

| Grade | Freedom to act |
|---|---|
| E | Works with some guidance within clearly defined objectives and established methods |
| F | Works independently within defined objectives and established frameworks |
| G | Leads and defines approaches within broad project objectives |
| H | Sets technical direction within major projects or capability areas |
| I | Exercises authoritative judgement within broad programme and organisational objectives |
| J | Sets strategic direction across major programmes, portfolios or organisational capabilities |
| K | Shapes organisation-level and external direction in a specialist field |

## 5. Preferred Accountability Verbs

| Grade | Preferred verbs |
|---|---|
| E | Apply, implement, contribute, support, interpret, record, compare, improve |
| F | Independently select, adapt, deliver, resolve, develop, take responsibility for |
| G | Define, lead, coordinate, evaluate, approve within scope, develop |
| H | Establish, direct, integrate, assure, resolve, set technical direction |
| I | Shape, lead, sponsor, authorise, determine within delegated scope, influence |
| J | Direct, create, prioritise, allocate, govern, shape strategy, establish |
| K | Originate, define, sponsor, influence, represent, set field direction |

## 6. Typical Stakeholder Reach

| Grade | Typical stakeholders |
|---|---|
| E | Immediate team, senior colleagues, laboratory colleagues, internal software users |
| F | Project teams, internal users, test engineers, partner specialists |
| G | Multidisciplinary project teams, work-package owners, internal capability users, external collaborators |
| H | Major programmes, capability leads, facility owners, senior technical stakeholders, partner organisations |
| I | Divisional leadership, major programme sponsors, senior technical authorities, funders, strategic partners |
| J | Organisational leadership, programme directors, major funders, government, industry and academic partners |
| K | Executive leadership, national and international bodies, industry, academia and the wider field |

## 7. Accountability Construction Rule

Each accountability should make the following visible:

1. **Input or starting point** — requirements, data, models, test objectives, workflows,
   technical problems or stakeholder needs.
2. **Action** — what the role holder does.
3. **Complexity and precedent** — how difficult, variable or novel the work is.
4. **Freedom to act** — the level of guidance, independence or delegated authority.
5. **Output or outcome** — the tangible result.
6. **Stakeholders or beneficiaries** — who uses, relies on or is affected by the result.

A useful sentence pattern is:

> **Action** + **input/object** + **complexity/precedent** + **freedom to act** +
> **tangible outcome** + **stakeholder/beneficiary**.

## 8. Specialist Route Principle

All role holders must demonstrate the grade-appropriate **core experimental-design
competence**. They must also demonstrate grade-appropriate capability in one or more
specialist areas, which may be computational, experimental or an integrated combination.

Equal depth across computational and experimental specialisms is not required. An
approximately 80/20 balance in either direction is acceptable at every grade, provided the
role holder maintains sufficient breadth to integrate digital and physical evidence.

Examples of advanced specialist skills include:

- scientific Python and numerical analysis;
- compiled and systems programming languages;
- profiling, benchmarking and performance-aware computing;
- vectorisation, threading, distributed computing and accelerator programming;
- sensor and measurement-system modelling;
- data-acquisition architecture and signal conditioning;
- advanced diagnostics such as digital image correlation, infrared thermography,
  high-speed imaging or photogrammetry;
- calibration, uncertainty analysis and engineering validation;
- optimisation, design of experiments and sensitivity analysis.

## 9. Terms to Avoid or Reserve

- Avoid **deep** before Grade H.
- Avoid **authoritative** before Grade I.
- Avoid **strategic** before Grade I except where describing the wider context rather than
  the role holder's authority.
- Avoid **internationally recognised** before Grade J or K.
- Avoid **field-defining** except at Grade K.
- Avoid stacking adjectives such as “advanced, deep, extensive and comprehensive” in one
  requirement.
- Avoid describing every computational and experimental skill as individually mandatory.
