# JD Jargon Progression: Experimental Design Engineer Grades E–K

This controlled vocabulary is intended to make progression between grades explicit and
consistent across the Experimental Design Engineer job family. It should be used in
accountabilities, qualifications and experience requirements without stacking several
near-synonyms in the same sentence.

## 1. Knowledge and Experience Progression

| Grade | Knowledge depth | Knowledge breadth | Experience depth | Experience breadth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | Demonstrable | Developing | Practical | Relevant |
| F | Strong | Growing | Established | Broader |
| G | Advanced | Broad | Substantial | Wide |
| H | Deep | Extensive | Extensive | Multidisciplinary |
| I | Authoritative | Comprehensive | Sustained and significant | Integrated and strategic |
| J | Strategic and authoritative | Organisation-wide | Extensive and sustained | Cross-programme and organisational |
| K | Internationally recognised authority | Field-shaping | Distinguished and sustained | National and international |

### Preferred grade-level phrases

- **E:** Demonstrable knowledge and practical experience, with developing breadth.
- **F:** Strong knowledge and established experience, with growing breadth.
- **G:** Advanced knowledge and substantial experience, with broad capability.
- **H:** Deep knowledge and extensive experience, with multidisciplinary breadth.
- **I:** Authoritative knowledge and sustained, significant experience, with
  comprehensive and integrated breadth.
- **J:** Strategic and authoritative knowledge with extensive, sustained leadership
  experience across programmes and organisational capabilities.
- **K:** Internationally recognised authority with distinguished, sustained experience
  shaping the field nationally and internationally.

## 3. Precedent and Novelty Progression

| Grade | Degree of precedent | Typical novelty |
|---|---|---|
| E | Clear precedent and established methods | Applies and implements |
| F | Established precedent requiring interpretation or adaptation | Selects and adapts |
| G | Partial precedent; established methods may not fully fit | Defines and develops |
| H | Limited precedent; significant method development required | Integrates and innovates |
| I | Limited or emerging precedent; novel integration often required | Establishes and shapes |
| J | Little precedent across programmes or organisational capability | Creates and directs |
| K | Little or no precedent nationally or internationally | Originates and defines the field |

## 7. Accountability Construction Rule

Each accountability should make the following visible:

1. **Input or starting point** — requirements, data, models, test objectives, workflows,
   technical problems or stakeholder needs.
2. **Action** — what the role holder does.
3. **Complexity and precedent** — how difficult, variable or novel the work is.
4. **Freedom to act** — the level of guidance, independence or delegated authority.
5. **Output or outcome** — the tangible result.
6. **Stakeholders or beneficiaries** — who uses, relies on or is affected by the result.

A useful sentence pattern is:

> **Action** + **input/object** + **complexity/precedent** + **freedom to act** +
> **tangible outcome** + **stakeholder/beneficiary**.

## 8. Specialist Route Principle

All role holders must demonstrate the grade-appropriate **core experimental-design
competence**. They must also demonstrate grade-appropriate capability in one or more
specialist areas, which may be computational, experimental or an integrated combination.

Equal depth across computational and experimental specialisms is not required. An
approximately 80/20 balance in either direction is acceptable at every grade, provided the
role holder maintains sufficient breadth to integrate digital and physical evidence.

Examples of advanced specialist skills include:

- scientific Python and numerical analysis;
- compiled and systems programming languages;
- profiling, benchmarking and performance-aware computing;
- vectorisation, threading, distributed computing and accelerator programming;
- sensor and measurement-system modelling;
- data-acquisition architecture and signal conditioning;
- advanced diagnostics such as digital image correlation, infrared thermography,
  high-speed imaging or photogrammetry;
- calibration, uncertainty analysis and engineering validation;
- optimisation, design of experiments and sensitivity analysis.

## 9. Terms to Avoid or Reserve

- Avoid **deep** before Grade H.
- Avoid **authoritative** before Grade I.
- Avoid **strategic** before Grade I except where describing the wider context rather than
  the role holder's authority.
- Avoid **internationally recognised** before Grade J or K.
- Avoid **field-defining** except at Grade K.
- Avoid stacking adjectives such as “advanced, deep, extensive and comprehensive” in one
  requirement.
- Avoid describing every computational and experimental skill as individually mandatory.
## 10. Accountability Axis Progression

The accountability axis should be expressed through six linked dimensions:

1. **Magnitude of output** — the scale of work for which the role is accountable.
2. **Nature of impact** — whether the role contributes to, delivers, owns, governs or
   shapes outcomes.
3. **Decision authority** — what the role may decide, approve, recommend or escalate.
4. **Resource accountability** — the scale of people, budget, facilities, computing and
   equipment the role coordinates or influences.
5. **Planning horizon** — the duration over which the role plans and remains accountable.
6. **Risk and consequence** — the significance of technical, delivery, financial,
   reputational or strategic consequences.

### Accountability progression by grade

| Grade | Magnitude of output | Nature of impact | Decision authority | Resource accountability | Planning horizon | Risk and consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | Defined tasks and technical outputs | Contributes to reliable team and project outputs | Applies established methods; recommends improvements; escalates departures | Uses assigned computing, equipment and test time efficiently | Days to months | Reduces rework, local delay, data-quality problems and unreliable outputs |
| F | Complete components and defined studies | Delivers complete outcomes within defined objectives | Selects and adapts methods independently; resolves routine departures | Plans resources for defined studies and activities | Months | Protects study quality, project decisions and delivery commitments |
| G | Work packages and specialist capability components | Owns work-package outcomes and technical quality within scope | Defines approaches and approves outputs within assigned work packages | Manages allocated work-package resources and coordinates contributors | Several months to approximately one year | Manages technical, delivery and interface risk across multidisciplinary work |
| H | Major projects and defined capability areas | Is accountable for coordinated project or capability outcomes | Sets technical direction and acts as delegated approver within assigned scope | Coordinates substantial people, facilities, computing and experimental resources across projects | Annual and multiyear | Protects major programme, facility and multidisciplinary outcomes |
| I | One significant capability or strategically important project area | Is accountable for significant capability outcomes and programme evidence | Makes and approves significant decisions within delegated capability authority | Plans and prioritises substantial resources within one capability or project area | Multiyear capability roadmap | Influences significant programme evidence, capability investment and organisational risk |
| J | Major multiyear organisational function or capability portfolio | Directs and governs functional or portfolio outcomes | Sets priorities, governs approaches and allocates resources across a major function | Allocates and reallocates substantial resources across programmes and capabilities | Long-term function and portfolio direction | Affects organisational priorities, substantial resources and long-term capability |
| K | Organisation-level and field-level technical direction | Shapes the organisation’s long-term technical position and the wider field | Provides organisation-level authoritative judgement and shapes external research direction | Influences major strategic investment through technical authority rather than necessarily managing it directly | Long-term organisational and field direction | Affects organisational reputation, strategically critical programmes and field direction |

### Preferred accountability verbs and phrases

| Grade | Accountability keywords |
|---|---|
| E | Contributes to; applies; implements; supports; recommends; escalates; produces reliable outputs; reduces rework; improves consistency; uses assigned resources efficiently |
| F | Delivers; independently selects; adapts; resolves; takes responsibility for; completes defined studies; improves reliability; plans study resources; reduces repeated effort |
| G | Owns; defines; leads; coordinates; approves within scope; manages allocated resources; delivers accepted work-package outcomes; reduces technical risk |
| H | Is accountable for; establishes; directs; integrates; assures; acts as delegated approver; coordinates substantial resources; resolves conflicting requirements; protects major outcomes |
| I | Leads and is accountable for; shapes; authorises within delegated scope; prioritises; sponsors; exercises authoritative judgement; delivers capability outcomes; influences investment |
| J | Directs and governs; sets priorities; allocates and reallocates; establishes functional strategy; holds accountability for; secures commitment; delivers organisational outcomes |
| K | Shapes; originates; defines the field; provides organisation-level authority; influences strategic investment; establishes external direction; protects long-term reputation and capability |

## 11. Communication and Influencing Progression

| Grade | Communication and influencing challenge |
|---|---|
| E | Explains methods and accurately reports results to immediate technical stakeholders |
| F | Advises project colleagues and adapts communication for different technical audiences |
| G | Influences multidisciplinary decisions and builds agreement across technical interfaces |
| H | Resolves conflicting technical positions and builds consensus across projects and partner organisations |
| I | Provides authoritative advice and influences senior programme and capability decisions |
| J | Negotiates priorities and secures organisational and external commitment across a major function |
| K | Shapes agendas and persuades senior authorities and international peers to pursue new directions |

### Communication keywords

- **E:** explain, report, document, present clearly, support understanding.
- **F:** advise, adapt communication, justify, recommend, secure agreement within a project.
- **G:** influence, build agreement, defend recommendations, resolve interfaces.
- **H:** build consensus, reconcile competing positions, negotiate technical priorities.
- **I:** provide authoritative advice, influence senior decisions, shape capability choices.
- **J:** negotiate organisational priorities, secure commitment, represent the function.
- **K:** shape agendas, persuade international peers, define external direction, represent the field.

## 12. Nature-of-Impact Ladder

Use this ladder consistently to distinguish contribution from direct accountability:

> **Contributes to → Delivers → Owns → Is accountable for → Directs and governs → Shapes**

- **E:** contributes to reliable technical outputs.
- **F:** delivers complete technical components or studies.
- **G:** owns work-package outcomes.
- **H:** is accountable for coordinated project or capability outcomes.
- **I:** is accountable for a significant capability’s technical outcomes.
- **J:** directs and governs a major function or portfolio.
- **K:** shapes the organisation’s long-term technical position and the wider field.

Avoid using **owns**, **is accountable for**, **governs** or **shapes** below the grade at
which the role genuinely carries that level of authority.

## 13. Largest Accountable Output Progression

The largest accountable output is the primary accountability axis. Use exactly one term
at each grade and link decision authority, resource authority, impact and planning horizon
to that output.

| Grade | Largest accountable output | Impact term | Decision term | Resource term | Planning horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | Task | Contributes | Applies | Uses | Days to months |
| F | Study | Delivers | Selects | Plans | Months |
| G | Work package | Owns | Defines | Manages | Several months to one year |
| H | Project | Is accountable for | Sets | Coordinates | Annual to multiyear |
| I | Capability | Leads and is accountable for | Approves | Prioritises | Multiyear roadmap |
| J | Function | Directs and governs | Governs | Allocates | Long-term functional strategy |
| K | Field | Shapes | Influences | Influences investment | Long-term organisational and field direction |

Use this exact progression:

> **Task → Study → Work package → Project → Capability → Function → Field**

Preferred grade-level phrases:

- **E:** contributes to the delivery of a defined task.
- **F:** delivers a complete defined study.
- **G:** owns the delivery and technical quality of a work package.
- **H:** is accountable for the technical outcomes of a project.
- **I:** leads and is accountable for a significant capability.
- **J:** directs and governs a major organisational function.
- **K:** shapes the direction and development of a field.

Everything else in the accountability wording should be relative to this output. Decision
authority occurs within the resources allocated to achieve the defined output.

## 14. First-Three-Bullets Rule

The first three bullets in each major section should establish the three principal
Korn Ferry Hay axes before the section moves into detailed technical content.

### Accountabilities

The first three accountability bullets should cover:

1. **Know-how:** the grade-appropriate technical and managerial knowledge applied.
2. **Problem solving:** the complexity, precedent and novelty of the work.
3. **Accountability:** the largest accountable output, decision authority, resources,
   tangible outcome and affected stakeholders.

Where possible, combine all three axes in each of the first three bullets, while ensuring
that the grade's defining output term remains explicit.

### Essential Qualifications / Experience

The first three essential bullets after any factual degree requirement should cover:

1. **Know-how:** grade-appropriate depth and breadth of knowledge.
2. **Problem solving:** grade-appropriate experience of complexity, precedent and novelty.
3. **Accountability:** experience delivering the grade's defining output and exercising
   the corresponding level of judgement and responsibility.

### Desirable Qualifications / Experience

Order desirable criteria by:

1. specialist depth that strengthens the primary route;
2. breadth into a complementary computational or experimental route;
3. external, collaborative or leadership experience appropriate to the grade.

Use these terms consistently in the Overall Purpose, accountabilities and Essential
criteria.

| Grade | Specialist status | Advice wording |
|---|---|---|
| E | Developing subject-matter specialist | Provides specialist advice within the immediate team |
| F | Recognised subject-matter specialist | Provides specialist advice to project colleagues |
| G | Recognised senior specialist | Provides specialist advice across multidisciplinary projects |
| H | Subject-matter expert | Provides expert advice and technical strategic leadership across projects and partner organisations |
| I | Industry-recognised subject-matter expert | Provides authoritative specialist advice to programmes and senior stakeholders |
| J | Nationally and internationally recognised expert | Provides strategic technical advice to organisational leadership and external partners |
| K | Internationally recognised authority and organisation-wide go-to person | Advises senior leaders and national or international bodies and shapes the field |

Additional role-snapshot phrases should escalate as follows:

- **E:** proactively identifies opportunities and recommends improvements.
- **F:** reviews and approves defined technical outputs and mentors less experienced colleagues.
- **G:** assigns, coordinates and reviews contributors and may provide specialist training.
- **H:** provides technical strategic leadership and coordinates internal and external specialists.
- **I:** holds final technical sign-off within a capability and formulates technical policy and standards.
- **J:** acts as final technical authority for a function and governs policy and operating standards.
- **K:** serves as the organisation-wide go-to authority and represents the organisation on
  national or international advisory bodies.

## 16. Input–Output–Stakeholder Accountability Rule

Every accountability must describe an accountable result rather than an activity alone.
Each bullet should contain:

1. **Input:** the requirement, objective, evidence, data, problem, constraint or resource
   from which the role starts.
2. **Action and judgement:** the grade-appropriate action, complexity, precedent and
   decision authority.
3. **Tangible output:** a method, plan, dataset, implementation, approval, evidence
   package, recommendation, policy, capability, agreement or other reviewable result.
4. **Stakeholder scope:** the people, teams, programmes, partners, organisation or field
   that use or are affected by the output.

Preferred construction:

> Convert / translate / apply / review **[input]** using **[grade-appropriate judgement]**
> into **[tangible output]** for **[stakeholder scope and intended effect]**.

Avoid bullets that end after verbs such as *lead*, *support*, *coordinate*, *advise*,
*develop* or *influence* without stating the resulting output and affected stakeholders.
